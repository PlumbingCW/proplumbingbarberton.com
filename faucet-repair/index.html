Emergency Faucet Repair in Barberton, OH

A dripping faucet wastes water, but a supply valve that will not shut off is an emergency. We repair faucets, valves and sink plumbing and answer 24/7.

A valve that will not shut off

If a faucet or supply line is spraying and the valve under the sink will not close, shut off the main water valve and call. Our dispatch is open 24/7, and while you wait you can put a bucket under the leak and move cabinet contents.

A burst braided supply line is a common cause of sudden flooding. If the main valve also sticks, tell the dispatcher so we bring the right parts.

Fixture problems we repair

Faucets fail in a few recognizable ways.

  • ✓Drip from the spout when the handle is off
  • ✓Leak at the base of the handle or around the spout
  • ✓Low flow or uneven spray from the aerator
  • ✓Tub faucet that drips or will not turn off
  • ✓Shower valve that leaks or will not mix hot and cold
  • ✓Supply line or shutoff valve that weeps

Why hard water wears fixtures faster

Barberton's latest water quality report lists hardness around 170 mg/L. Minerals collect in aerators, cartridges and shower heads, which cuts flow and gums up moving parts.

Soaking an aerator in vinegar clears light buildup. If the handle is stiff or the cartridge sticks, a replacement saves frustration. For ongoing scale, consider a water softener.

Kitchen and bathroom sinks

The supply lines under a sink are a frequent culprit. Braided lines age, and the nuts loosen over years of cabinet traffic. We replace worn lines and valves while we are there, so you do not call back in a month.

If water drains slowly after the faucet is fixed, the trap or the line may be partly blocked, and drain cleaning is the next step.

Repair or replace the faucet

A faucet that is corroded, pitted or short of parts is cheaper to replace than to repair. A fixture you love with a failed cartridge is worth repairing.

We can install the new faucet you bought or recommend one, and larger bathroom work goes to our plumbing installation team.

Choosing a replacement faucet

Match the new faucet to the holes in your sink or counter. A single-hole faucet fits a three-hole sink with a deck plate, but a three-hole faucet cannot fit a single-hole sink.

Look at the valve inside, not just the finish. Faucets with ceramic disc cartridges tend to hold up better in hard water than older washer designs, and a brand with easy-to-find cartridges makes the next repair cheap.

What to do before we arrive

Close the small shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If the valves are stuck or leak, shut off the main water valve instead.

Wipe up water around the cabinet and check the floor for soft spots. Do not force a seized handle, because a snapped stem can turn a drip into a spray.

Water-saving standards for new faucets

The EPA WaterSense label covers bathroom faucets that flow at 1.5 gallons per minute or less. If a faucet is past repair, a labeled replacement can cut water use without changing how it feels.
