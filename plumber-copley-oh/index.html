Start with the map: where Copley sits

Copley Township had 18,403 residents in the 2020 census. It borders Akron to the east, Fairlawn to the northeast and Norton to the south, with a township hall at 1540 S. Cleveland-Massillon Road.

The Barberton Reservoir is among the township's landmarks, and Copley Circle with its gazebo is a well-known center. Those landmarks help when you describe your location to the dispatcher.

Check who supplies your water

Water service in Copley is not the same everywhere. The City of Akron's water pages say Akron serves Copley Township only in part, and other addresses may use different supply arrangements.

The simplest check is your bill. Knowing your supplier tells you who to call when water goes off, and it tells you which water quality report applies to your tap. Summit County Public Health's Water Quality Lab offers drinking water testing if you want a result for your house.

If your pipe bursts in Copley

Emergency dispatch to Copley runs around the clock. Do these four things first while you wait.

1

Close the main valve

Turn the main shutoff to the closed position, then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines.

2

Protect the electric

Stay out of standing water near outlets, and switch off breakers only if the panel is dry.

3

Save what you can

Move furniture, boxes and electronics out of reach of the water.

4

Phone the dispatcher

Give your address, the pipe location and whether the water has stopped.

Sewer lines on larger Copley lots

A property set back from the road on a larger lot often has a longer lateral from house to main. A longer run has more joints, more room for roots to enter and more distance for a blockage to hide.

If a drain keeps backing up, sewer line cleaning clears the immediate blockage and a camera inspection shows the cause. Properties on septic or private systems differ, so tell the dispatcher what you have.

Leaks, bills and hard-to-find water

A water bill that jumps or a meter that keeps moving points to a leak. On a larger property the leak may be in the yard, between the meter and the house, where nothing shows on the surface.

Our leak detection team uses acoustic listening and thermal imaging to find it. When a pipe has already failed, the steps on the burst pipe repair page apply.

Planned work: heaters and hard water in Copley

Water hardness depends on the supplier, so do not assume the Barberton figure applies at your tap. Before you size a softener or plan a tankless install, check your bill to see who supplies you and ask for the latest water quality report, or send a sample to a lab.

Once you know the number, a new water heater or a water softener can be sized properly. We would rather size from a real result than from a neighbor's advice.

Where to check your water standards

Water suppliers in Ohio publish an annual consumer confidence report as the EPA requires, and the report lists hardness, lead and copper results. Because the supplier varies by address in Copley, look at the report from the utility on your bill.

Plumbing work in the township follows the Ohio Plumbing Code, which builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
