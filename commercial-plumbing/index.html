When plumbing stops, a business stops. We handle after-hours backups, leaks and heater failures for small and mid-size commercial properties, and dispatch answers 24/7.

After-hours commercial plumbing emergency

A backup in a restaurant kitchen or a leak above a stockroom cannot wait for Monday. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and while you wait you should close the main valve to the affected area, stop using the fixtures and move stock and equipment away from water.

Tell the dispatcher the type of business, the fixture or line involved and whether customers are in the building. That helps us send the right equipment on the first trip.

What we do not take on

We do not do engineered fire sprinkler systems, medical gas or large new-build commercial plumbing from plans. Those need specialist contractors and permits that run through an engineer.

Grease, backflow and the water utility

Grease interceptor pumping is normally done by a licensed hauler. We handle the plumbing side, including the lines leading to the interceptor, jetting and repairs. Ask your hauler how often to pump, and keep the receipts, because inspectors may ask.

Backflow assembly testing must be done by a certified tester and reported to the water utility. Barberton's water pages list a backflow contact, so confirm requirements for your property. Tell us what device you have and we will say plainly whether we can handle it.

Scheduled maintenance beats midnight calls

Food-service lines do best on a set jetting schedule, and the right interval depends on volume. A busy kitchen may need it quarterly, while a small cafe may need it twice a year.

Ask about a maintenance visit that also checks floor drains, hot water temperature and restroom fixtures. A short walkthrough before the busy season often finds a slow drain or a weeping valve that would have failed on a Friday night.

Codes and rules that apply to businesses

Commercial work falls under the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including fixture counts, backflow protection and grease interceptors where food is prepared. The local sewer authority sets its own rules for grease and discharge, so we confirm those before a restaurant job.

Ask your landlord who owns the plumbing behind the wall. That one answer often decides who pays.
