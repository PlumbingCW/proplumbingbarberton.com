Tankless Water Heater Service in Barberton, OH

A tankless heater heats water on demand and saves floor space. In hard water it needs regular descaling, which is where most problems start.

What a tankless water heater does

A tankless unit heats water as it passes through a heat exchanger, so there is no storage tank to leak or run out. That gives you steady hot water while the unit has enough capacity for the fixtures running.

These units are not maintenance free. Mineral scale clogs the heat exchanger over time, and the fix is a descale flush. If you are still comparing tank types, see our water heater installation page.

Why hard water matters for tankless

Barberton's latest water quality report lists hardness at about 170 mg/L, with a range of 100 to 250. That is hard water, and it leaves scale on the inside of a tankless heat exchanger.

  • ✓Error codes for flame failure or low flow
  • ✓Hot water that fluctuates during a shower
  • ✓Slow rise in water temperature
  • ✓Higher fuel use than before
  • ✓A rumbling or whistling sound from the unit

Left alone, scale cuts flow and efficiency and can shorten the unit's life. A yearly flush is cheap compared with a heat exchanger replacement.

How a descale flush works

We pump a mild descaling solution through the unit in a closed loop.

    Isolate the unit

    We close the service valves and connect hoses to the flush ports.

  2. 2

    Circulate the solution

    A pump moves descaling solution through the heat exchanger.

  3. 3

    Rinse and clean the filter

    We flush with clean water and clear the inlet screen.

  4. 4

    Test the unit

    We restore flow, check temperatures and clear any error codes.

If your unit lacks service valves, we can add them so future flushes are quick.

Repairs we do

Common tankless faults include failed igniters, flame sensors, flow sensors and condensate drain clogs. Many show up as an error code on the display, which helps us pick the right part.

If the unit has failed past repair, our replacement service can swap it for a new one. We tell you plainly when repair costs approach the price of a new unit.

Venting, gas and electrical needs

A tankless gas unit often needs a larger gas line and special venting than a tank heater, and an electric unit can need a heavy circuit. We check these before quoting, because a good unit on a poor gas line gives poor results.

Sizing a tankless unit for an Ohio winter

A tankless heater is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat by a given temperature rise. Incoming water is colder in a northern Ohio winter, so the same unit delivers less flow in January than it does in July.

That is why we size from your coldest water and your busiest hour, not from a showroom label. Two showers and a dishwasher at once is a normal morning, and a unit that handles it in winter will handle it all year.

Venting and gas guidelines

A gas tankless unit needs venting and a gas line sized according to the manufacturer's instructions and the codes that apply to the job. An undersized gas line is one of the most common reasons a new unit shuts down during a hot shower.

Hard water near 170 mg/L means a tankless heat exchanger collects scale faster, so a yearly descaling flush belongs in the maintenance plan.
