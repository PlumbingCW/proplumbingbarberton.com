A mixed district, not a subdivision

Downtown Barberton is a business and mixed-use area and not a typical residential neighborhood. The city's own materials describe the downtown area as an Arts and Entertainment District.

A neighborhood guide places shops, bars and a movie theater along West Tuscarawas Avenue, a farmers market on Tuesdays and groceries at Magic City Plaza. Lake Anna Park sits at the heart of the city and hosts many events.

Who calls from downtown

Restaurant and bar owners, shop owners, landlords with apartments above stores and tenants in older mixed-use buildings all call a plumber. The first question is often who is responsible, so check your lease before you call.

If you run a business, read about commercial plumbing. If it is an apartment, the landlord usually handles the main lines and the tenant the fixtures, but leases differ.

Grease, drains and backflow

A kitchen drain carries grease and food that can build up in the line, and a repeat clog is a sign to look further. Hydro jetting cleans pipe walls more thoroughly than a cable alone.

Some businesses also need a backflow preventer on certain connections. Barberton's water department lists a backflow contact, so ask it what applies at your address.

Permits and the building department

Barberton's building department has handled plumbing permits, and in 2026 the city announced that Summit County's Division of Building Standards takes over state-required code enforcement. Plumbing permits are not named in that notice, so we ask the right office before work on anything that may need one.

That matters for tenants and landlords because unpermitted work can cause trouble when a building is sold or inspected.

Planning around business hours

A restaurant cannot stay closed for long, so tell us your hours when you call. We will explain what can be done and how long it is likely to take, and we say so honestly when something needs a second visit.
