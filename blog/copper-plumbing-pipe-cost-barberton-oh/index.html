Copper Plumbing Pipe Cost in Barberton, OH

Copper pipe is priced by the foot, but the job is priced by fittings, labor and access. In Barberton, where the water is hard but not aggressive, copper remains a durable choice, though PEX is often cheaper to install.

By the Barberton Pro Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Why price per foot is only part of the story

Copper pipe is sold by the length, and the price follows the copper market, so it moves from month to month. The pipe is only one part of the bill, because every joint needs a fitting, solder and a skilled hand with a torch.

Labor is where copper differs most from PEX. Copper must be cut, cleaned, fluxed and soldered at each joint. PEX uses crimp or expansion fittings and flexible tubing that bends around corners, so it often installs faster.

Copper types and where they are used

Copper pipe comes in thickness grades.

Copper pipe types
TypeWall thicknessCommon use
Type KThickestUnderground service lines and heavy duty
Type LMediumInterior supply lines in most homes
Type MThinnerInterior supply where code allows

Thicker walls cost more per foot. Check what your local code allows before choosing a grade.

What changes the total cost

The number of fixtures, the length of runs and how many walls must be opened are the big drivers. A house with a basement and open joists is cheaper to pipe than one with plaster walls and finished ceilings.

Fittings add up too. Elbows, tees, valves and unions each cost more than a straight length, so a complicated layout costs more than a straight run of the same length.

Barberton local note

Does Barberton's water suit copper?

Barberton's latest drinking water results list a pH of 7.30 and a copper 90th percentile of 0.2965 parts per million, well below the 1.3 parts per million action level. That is a utility-wide result, not a guarantee for any one house.

Hardness is about 170 mg/L, so scale will form in any metal pipe over the years. Copper handles that well when installed correctly, and a water heater flush plus a water softener reduce the scale load.

Source: City of Barberton consumer confidence report.

When PEX makes more sense

PEX resists freeze splitting better than rigid pipe and installs faster, so a full house repipe can cost less. Copper has a longer track record and handles higher temperatures near heat sources.

Many plumbers mix the two: copper at the water heater and in exposed runs, PEX in the walls. Ask for both options in writing so you compare like with like. Our repiping page explains how a full replacement works.

Planning for pinhole leaks

If your copper lines have started to pinhole, the cause may be local corrosion, high water velocity or a poorly made joint. One pinhole may be a repair, while several in a year suggest a section is failing.

Find out where the leaks start with leak detection, then decide whether to patch or replace. A burst section needs fast action, and burst pipe repair covers the first steps.

Soldering, fittings and code

Modern copper joints use lead-free solder, and fittings must meet the plumbing code. Poor soldering leads to pinhole leaks and weeping joints years later, so craftsmanship matters more than the price per foot.

Ask whether the plumber uses press fittings, which join copper without a torch. They cost more per fitting but can speed the job and avoid open flame near framing.

Copper compared with other materials over time

PEX and CPVC cost less to install, and copper costs more but has a long record in homes. Each has failure modes: copper can pinhole in aggressive water, PEX can fail at poor fittings and CPVC can turn brittle with age.

Choose by your house, your water and your budget. The cheapest option today is not always the cheapest over twenty years.

How to compare quotes fairly

Ask each plumber to list the pipe grade, the fitting type, the number of fixtures served, the permit fee and who patches the walls. A low quote that excludes wall repair may cost more in the end.

Request a written scope with a start date and a completion estimate. A clear scope is a good sign of a careful contractor.

What to expect during the job

A copper repipe is loud, dusty and takes planning. The crew shuts off water, cuts out old lines, runs new pipe and tests under pressure. Expect water to be off for part of each day and walls to be opened where the route requires it.

Ask how the crew protects floors and furniture, and who patches the drywall. A clear plan for cleanup is part of a fair price.

Recycling and old pipe

Old copper has scrap value, and some plumbers credit it against the job. Ask whether the quote accounts for it. Old galvanized steel has no such value and goes to the dumpster.

Pipe standards behind the price

Copper tube is sold in types K, L and M under ASTM B88, and the wall thickness explains much of the price gap between them. The Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), decides which type is allowed where.

Getting a fair quote

Ask for the pipe grade, the fitting type, the permit fee and the wall restoration scope. Compare totals, not per-foot prices.
