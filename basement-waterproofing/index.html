Basement Waterproofing in Barberton, OH

A damp basement ruins stored belongings and invites mold. We install interior drain systems with a sump pump to move groundwater out before it reaches the floor.

Why Barberton basements take on water

Barberton's original settlement sits in a valley beside the Tuscarawas River, with creeks feeding in from the surrounding slopes. When the ground is saturated, water presses against foundation walls and finds any crack or joint.

The June 2019 flooding, which followed nearly a month of heavy rain, showed how fast conditions can change here. Local officials also note that parts of the city are served by combined sewers, so storm flows can add pressure to the system during big storms.

What a waterproofing system includes

An interior system uses a channel or drain pipe around the inside perimeter of the basement floor that collects water and sends it to a sump pit. A sump pump then discharges it away from the house.

We install the pit, pump, check valve, discharge line and a battery backup when needed. Our sump pump service covers the pump side in detail.

What we do and do not do

We install interior drainage and sump systems, which is plumbing work. Structural crack repair, exterior excavation and foundation underpinning belong to a foundation specialist.

If your basement has bowing walls or large horizontal cracks, get a structural opinion before you waterproof. We will tell you plainly when a problem is beyond our scope.

Moisture, mold and what to do in the meantime

A damp basement raises humidity through the whole house. Run a dehumidifier, keep stored items off the floor and on shelves, and direct downspouts several feet away from the foundation while you plan permanent work.

Mold grows on materials that stay wet for days. Our work addresses the water source, and a mold specialist handles remediation. If you see widespread growth or anyone in the home has breathing trouble, ask a medical professional and a mold specialist, since mold health questions are outside plumbing.

What the guidelines say about wet basements

The EPA advises drying wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours to head off mold growth. That is why a basement that stays damp for days deserves a firm answer on cause, not another coat of sealer.

Interior drains, sump pumps and sealants each solve a different problem. We tell you which one fits the water you actually have.
