Repiping in Barberton, OH

When old pipes keep springing leaks, patching stops making sense. Repiping replaces the supply lines so water runs clear, with steady pressure and fewer repairs.

Call (855) 575-2890

Signs your home needs repiping

A single leak is a repair. A pattern of leaks, rusty water and falling pressure is a sign the whole line is wearing out. If this is your situation, plan ahead so a failure does not choose the day for you.

If water is actively leaking now, shut off the main and read about burst pipe repair. This page is for planned replacement.

Warning signs to watch

These point to old supply lines near the end of life.

  • ✓Brown or rust-tinted water, especially after the tap sits unused
  • ✓Pinhole leaks that keep reappearing
  • ✓Low pressure that gets worse over the years
  • ✓Visible green or white crust on copper, or flaking on galvanized steel
  • ✓Pipes that clang or vibrate when water runs
  • ✓A home built before modern plastic piping that has never been repiped

Galvanized pipe and Barberton homes

Barberton was founded in 1891, and its population peaked at 33,805 in 1960, so many neighborhoods have homes from the decades when galvanized steel and copper were the standard. If your house still has gray steel supply lines, scale inside them narrows the pipe over decades.

Hard water adds to the story. The city's latest water quality report lists hardness around 170 mg/L, so scale can form on the inside of older pipe. Replacing the old line restores flow in a way no cleaning does.

A note on lead

Barberton's latest drinking water results show a lead 90th percentile of about 4 parts per billion against a 15 parts per billion action level, with none of 31 sampled homes over. That is a utility-wide result and says nothing about a specific house.

Homes with older solder or service lines can differ. If you are concerned, ask the city utilities office about testing, and do not assume your own plumbing matches the city average. Replacing old supply lines is one way to remove the question.

How a repipe works

A good repipe is planned to limit mess.

  1. 1

    Survey the house

    We trace the supply lines, find access points and plan the route.

  2. 2

    Choose the material

    We recommend PEX or copper based on your house and budget.

  3. 3

    Replace in sections

    We open only the walls and ceilings needed and install new lines.

  4. 4

    Test and restore

    We pressure test the new lines and leave access areas ready for drywall repair.

Living through a repipe

Plan on water being off for part of each working day, so fill a few containers for drinking and flushing and use a neighbor or family member for showers if the schedule is tight. We tell you in advance when each stretch of the house will be down.

The plumbing crew opens walls only where needed, and a drywall finisher and painter handle the patches. Ask us for a list of access points so you can move furniture and clear closets before the first morning.

Partial repipes and permits

You do not always need the whole house. Replacing one failing run, such as a basement trunk line, is a common first step. Ask about it when you call, and see leak detection if you are unsure where the leaks start.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Barberton building department or Summit County before work starts. We also repipe homes in Norton and Copley.

Repeated leaks, rusty water and low pressure together point to a full replacement. One leak in a sound system is a repair.
