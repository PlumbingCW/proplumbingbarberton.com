Emergency jetting for a line that keeps backing up

A line that clogs, clears and clogs again is a good candidate for jetting, especially if water is already rising in a tub or floor drain. Our dispatch is open 24/7 and same-day visits are common, and until we arrive you should keep water use to a minimum and shut off the washer and dishwasher.

Move stored items away from basement drains. If sewage has reached the floor, stay off it and keep children and pets out of the room.

Cable or jetter: how to choose

A cable machine bores a hole through a blockage and leaves a coating of grease on the pipe wall. A jetter fires water through a nozzle that spins and scrubs the full circumference of the pipe. Neither tool is better in every case, so we pick by what the pipe needs.

A first-time hair clog in a tub trap is a cable job. A kitchen drain line with years of fat on its walls, or a main with scale and fine root hairs, is a jetting job.

Why Barberton lines build scale and grease

The city draws its water from the Barberton Reservoir, and its latest water quality report lists hardness around 170 mg/L, which is in the hard range. Hard water leaves mineral film inside pipes over the years, and that film gives grease something to grip.

Food-service kitchens feel this first because fat and starch arrive in volume. Homes feel it in the kitchen line that backs up at the same joint over and over.

How a jetting visit works

Jetting is a controlled process, not just a big hose.

  1. 1

    Camera or cable probe

    We check the pipe condition first, because jetting a cracked line can make the damage worse.

  2. 2

    Pick the nozzle

    A forward-cutting nozzle opens a plugged line. A rotating nozzle then scrubs the wall on the way back.

  3. 3

    Work the line in passes

    We jet from the cleanout toward the main, flushing debris ahead of us.

  4. 4

    Verify with water and a look

    We run flow to prove the line is clear and recommend a camera pass when the pipe is old.

When we say no to jetting

We will not jet a pipe that is collapsed, badly offset or held together by patches. Pressure can find the weak point and send debris and water where you do not want them.

In that case the better call is a sewer inspection followed by a sewer line repair. Saying so up front costs us a job and saves you a basement.

What to do before we arrive

Clear the area around floor drains and cleanouts so the plumber has room to work. Keep children and pets out of the basement and do not run water into the clogged line.

Tell the dispatcher if your sewer line is clay, cast iron or older, and whether roots or repeated backups are the problem. That helps the plumber choose the right jetting pressure.

Standards for jetting an old line

A camera check before and after jetting follows the pipeline coding method from NASSCO, the industry body for sewer inspection. Seeing the pipe wall first tells us whether jetting is safe or whether the line needs repair.
