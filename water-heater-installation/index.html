Planned work gives you time to compare fuel types, tank sizes and efficiency. It also lets us check the location, the venting, the shutoff valves and the drain path before the old unit fails.

What an install includes

We position the unit on a level base, connect hot and cold lines with shutoff valves, and fit a relief valve with a discharge pipe. Gas units get a vent connection and a gas shutoff, and electric units get a verified circuit.

Where space allows, we add an expansion tank on closed systems and a drain pan with a drain path. We then fill the tank, purge air, test for leaks and set the thermostat to a safe temperature.

Local consideration: hard water

Barberton's latest water quality report lists hardness around 170 mg/L, in the range most people call hard. A tank installed here will collect scale, so we plan for a drain valve at the bottom that is easy to reach for yearly flushing.

If you want to cut buildup further, ask about pairing the install with a water softener so the water arrives treated.

Permits and inspection

Many installations need a permit, and rules differ between the city and the county. Barberton's building department has handled plumbing permits, and the Summit County Division of Building Standards is taking over state code enforcement, so we confirm which office applies before we start.

The city's pages reference the 2017 Ohio Plumbing Code, and we install to the code in force on the day of the job.

Mistakes a careful install avoids

The most common problems we find on older installs are missing drain pans, undersized discharge pipes, no isolation valves and a venting run that sags. Each one is cheap to do right on the first visit and costly to fix after a leak.

We also avoid setting the thermostat too low or too high. A setting that is too low invites bacteria growth in the tank, and one that is too high raises scald risk, so we set a sensible temperature and explain how to adjust it. If a family member is young or elderly, tell us and we will plan for it.
