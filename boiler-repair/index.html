Emergency Boiler Repair in Barberton, OH

A boiler that quits in January is a safety problem, not just a comfort problem. We answer 24/7 and diagnose the fault, from a stuck zone valve to a failed circulator.

No heat: what to do first

A boiler failure in an Ohio winter can freeze pipes within hours in a cold house. Our dispatch is open 24/7, and while you wait you should check that the thermostat is calling, the boiler has power and the pressure gauge reads in the normal range printed on the unit.

If you smell gas, or the carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Do not restart the boiler until a professional has inspected it.

Signs of a boiler problem

Boilers fail in recognizable ways.

  • ✓No heat or uneven heat in some rooms
  • ✓Banging, kettling or gurgling noises
  • ✓Pressure that climbs or drops
  • ✓Water around the base of the boiler
  • ✓A burner that short-cycles or locks out
  • ✓A yellow or irregular flame on a gas boiler

Local winters and frozen pipes

Northeast Ohio winters can drop well below freezing. If the boiler is down for a long stretch, open cabinet doors on exterior walls, let a trickle run at the most exposed faucet, and call us.

If a pipe has already frozen or split, follow the steps on our burst pipe repair page and shut off the main water supply.

When replacement makes sense

A well-maintained boiler can last for decades, but an old unit with a cracked heat exchanger is not worth repairing. We tell you plainly when a repair is a stopgap.

A cracked heat exchanger or a failed combustion test is a safety issue, and we recommend replacement in those cases. For a planned swap, call to schedule a quote.

What a yearly boiler tune-up covers

A planned visit before the heating season costs far less than a January breakdown. We inspect the burner and heat exchanger, test the safety controls, check the expansion tank and pressure, and look for leaks and corrosion at valves and fittings.

We also test zone valves and the circulator, because those wear out quietly and fail on the coldest night. If the system has a flue or vent, we check that it is intact and clear, and we make sure carbon monoxide alarms are working in the home.

Boiler rules worth knowing in Ohio

Home boilers are not inspected on a schedule in Ohio, but boilers in many commercial and multifamily buildings fall under state boiler rules set by the Ohio Department of Commerce. If your building is more than a single-family home, ask whether an inspection certificate is due.

Repairs to a gas-fired boiler should follow the manufacturer's instructions and the Ohio Plumbing Code (built on the International Plumbing Code, IPC) and the mechanical code that apply to the job.
