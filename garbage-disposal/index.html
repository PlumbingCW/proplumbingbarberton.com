Disposal humming, jammed or leaking

If the disposal hums but will not spin, or water is pooling under the sink, switch the unit off at the wall switch and the breaker. Our dispatch is open 24/7, and while you wait you should keep your hands out of the unit and run no water into the sink.

Never put a hand in a disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs for anything stuck, and call us if it will not clear.

What the symptoms mean

The sound and the leak location tell us most of the story.

  • ✓Humming with no spin means a jam or a seized motor
  • ✓Silence at the switch means a tripped reset button or dead power
  • ✓Water from the bottom means the housing has failed
  • ✓Water from the top means a worn sink flange seal
  • ✓Water from the side means a loose drain or dishwasher hose connection
  • ✓A foul smell means trapped food or a slow drain line

Clearing a jam the safe way

This often works without a service call.

  1. 1

    Power off

    Turn off the switch and unplug or switch off the breaker.

  2. 2

    Use the hex socket

    Many units have a hex socket at the bottom for a wrench to free the flywheel.

  3. 3

    Reset

    Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.

  4. 4

    Test with water

    Run cold water and flip the switch.

If it still hums, the motor may be failing and we should look at it.

What goes down a Barberton kitchen drain

Disposal trouble often ends up in the drain line. Fat, coffee grounds and starchy peels settle, then harden into a paste that narrows the pipe. In a city with older lines and hard water, that paste builds up faster.

If the sink drains slowly even with the disposal running, the clog is past the unit. Our drain cleaning service clears it, and hydro jetting removes the grease coating for good.

Installing a new disposal

A straightforward swap takes about one to two hours. We remove the old unit, mount the new one, reconnect the drain and dishwasher hose, and wire it to a switch.

Wiring or a missing switch adds time, and a sink without a power outlet needs an electrician for the new circuit. We tell you before we start, and we can plan the work alongside other plumbing installation jobs.

Keeping a disposal healthy

Run cold water before, during and for several seconds after grinding, which keeps fats solid enough to move through the trap. Feed scraps in gradually instead of dumping a full bowl at once.

Skip bones, corn husks, artichoke leaves and anything fibrous. If the unit smells, a handful of ice and a splash of dish soap scrubs the chamber, and it costs nothing. A unit that smells even after cleaning often signals a drain-line problem, not a dirty disposal.

Installation and safety guidelines

A disposal must be installed according to the manufacturer's instructions, and its drain connection has to meet the Ohio Plumbing Code, which builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). That includes a proper trap and an air gap or high loop on the dishwasher drain where required.

Never put a hand in a disposal, even when it is off. Cut power at the breaker before any repair.
