What Barberton looks like from a plumber's truck

Barberton is a city of about 25,000 people on the Tuscarawas River, and the original settlement sits in a valley that runs parallel to the river and the old Ohio and Erie Canal. Lake Anna, a spring-fed lake in a 21-acre park, is one of its best-known landmarks.

That valley setting matters for plumbing. Creeks drain toward the low ground, and the city's June 2019 flooding showed how fast basements can take on water. If you live on lower streets, a working sump pump and a battery backup are worth planning before storm season.

The city's drinking water comes from the Barberton Reservoir, a 200-acre reservoir fed by the Upper Wolf Creek watershed, and is treated at the city plant. Hardness in the latest report is about 170 mg/L, so water heaters and fixtures collect scale, and a yearly flush belongs on your calendar.

How a dispatch works across the area

You call the 24/7 line and give your street. The dispatcher confirms the area, asks what is happening and books the next available plumber. For emergencies in any of these places the process is the same, and the price is stated before work starts.

If you live outside the places listed, call anyway. We would rather tell you honestly whether we can reach you than leave you searching in a crisis.

Nearby places we also take calls from

Coventry Township shares ZIP 44203 with Barberton, and part of it uses water from the Barberton plant. Addresses on the Akron side of Copley are within reach as well.

If your town is not listed, call anyway. The dispatcher will confirm whether a plumber can reach you and how long it will take.

Before you call from any of these places

Have your street address and the nearest cross street ready, because a dispatcher needs both to place you on the right side of a town line. Tell us what you see, such as water on the floor, a gurgling drain or a pilot light that will not stay lit.

If you can safely reach the shutoff, close it before you call. Most homes have a main water valve near the meter, often in the basement on the street side, and closing it can stop damage while you wait.

Why ZIP codes do not tell the whole story

ZIP codes and city limits do not always match. Norton and Barberton share 44203, and some Copley addresses use 44333 even though they sit in the township. Water and sewer providers follow their own maps, so the company that bills you may differ from the one your neighbor uses.

That matters for repairs. A problem on the public side of the meter or sewer connection belongs to the utility, and a problem on your side belongs to you. A plumber can usually tell which is which in a few minutes on site.

Seasonal work across the area

Winter brings frozen lines and boiler calls, and spring brings sump pump and basement water calls when the creeks rise. Summer storms add sewer backups in low streets, and fall is the right time to flush a water heater before the cold sets in.

A short checklist helps in any season. Test the sump pump, look at the water heater for rust or drips, and learn where your main shutoff sits. Our services page lists the work we do for each.
