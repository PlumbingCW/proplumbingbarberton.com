Emergency Sump Pump Repair and Installation in Barberton, OH

Basement taking on water: do this first

If water is rising in the sump pit or across the floor, call right away. Our dispatch is open 24/7, and while you wait you should unplug electrical items on the floor, move belongings up and stay out of water near outlets.

Check that the pump is plugged in, the breaker has not tripped and the float is free to rise. Do not touch an energized pump standing in water. If you cannot tell whether power is safe, wait for a plumber.

Signs your sump pump is failing

Pumps fail without much warning, so watch for these.

  • ✓The pump runs constantly or cycles rapidly
  • ✓It hums but does not move water
  • ✓Grinding, rattling or a rough sound
  • ✓The pit has water but the pump does not start
  • ✓The pump is more than ten years old
  • ✓The discharge line leaks or freezes in winter

Why a valley city needs a reliable pump

Barberton sits in a valley beside the Tuscarawas River with creeks draining toward it. After nearly a month of heavy rain, the June 2019 flooding put water into basements across the city, so a pump that works only on a dry day is not enough.

That is why we pair a primary pump with a battery backup when the home has a deep or flood-prone basement. Power often fails in the same storm that fills the pit.

Where the water goes

Sump discharge should go away from the house, not back to the foundation. Many municipalities prohibit connecting a sump to the sanitary sewer, so ask Barberton's utilities or building office what applies before you tie in.

The city's stormwater information also notes that construction in the 100-year floodplain needs a permit. If your property is low-lying, ask before changing drainage.

Test the pump before storm season

Pour a few gallons of water into the pit and watch the float rise and the pump start. The water should drop quickly, the discharge line should carry it outside and no water should run back down when the pump stops.

Check the battery backup at the same time. Batteries lose capacity with age, and a backup that has not been tested is a guess. Clear debris from the pit, keep the lid sealed if the pit has one and make sure the outdoor discharge end is not buried or blocked.
